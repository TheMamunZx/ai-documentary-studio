# Documentary Scriptwriting

## Audience and voice
Write narration for speaking, not for an academic paper. Default Hindi narration is in Devanagari unless the brief asks for another language. Use short, natural sentences; explain technical terms; keep a consistent narrator voice.

## Narrative architecture
A typical long-form arc: cold open with a truthful question or visual contrast → stakes and context → evidence and chronology → how it works → competing explanations/complications → present implications → calibrated conclusion.

## Rules
- Use only claim IDs approved by the claim register.
- Do not invent scenes, quotes, dialogue, motives, or private thoughts.
- Do not state hypotheses as facts or compress debate into certainty.
- Avoid fake cliffhangers, filler questions, keyword stuffing, and repetitive restatement.
- Use transitions that advance the explanation.
- Keep pronunciation notes in a separate glossary rather than cluttering spoken narration.
- Mark narration, on-screen text, source note, pause, and scene ID separately.
- Estimate runtime from a realistic speaking range, then replace estimates with measured voiceover durations.

## Deliverables
`master_script.md`, `chapter_outline.md`, `pronunciation_guide.md`, and `script_fact_map.csv` linking scene/paragraph IDs to claims and sources.
