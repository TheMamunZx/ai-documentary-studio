# Storyboard & Shot Planning

## Goal
Translate each narration beat into an intentional visual shot with a documented source type and measurable duration.

## Required fields
`shot_id, chapter, in_time, out_time, duration_seconds, narration_id, narration_text, shot_purpose, subject, action, setting_period, shot_size, camera_angle, camera_move, lens_feel, lighting, mood, palette, on_screen_text, visual_type, prompt_id, continuity_key, source_or_asset_id, generation_status, notes`.

## Workflow
1. Split the approved script by visual beats, not arbitrary sentence count.
2. Choose the best explanatory visual: archival, licensed stock, AI reconstruction, diagram, map, chart, screen capture, or typography.
3. Avoid generic filler footage that does not show the thing being discussed.
4. Mix establishing shots, medium shots, close detail, evidence close-ups, maps, diagrams, and restrained motion graphics.
5. Use shot length to match information density and narration cadence.
6. Add “do not depict” notes for anachronisms, unsupported events, and unverifiable details.
7. Keep a coverage report: every important narration statement should have a visual or explicit reason for remaining on-screen text.

## Handoff
Storyboard CSV, human-readable shot list, visual coverage report, and continuity keys. Storyboard timing is provisional until voiceover is measured.
