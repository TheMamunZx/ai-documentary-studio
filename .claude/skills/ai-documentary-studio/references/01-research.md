# Documentary Research Protocol

## Research objective
Create a source-backed dossier that can be audited and converted into narration without losing the distinction between evidence and interpretation.

## Source priority
1. Primary documents: official datasets, government or institutional reports, original papers, archival catalogues, court documents, recorded statements, technical specifications, and first-hand records.
2. Peer-reviewed research and established academic publishers.
3. Reputable journalism and specialist secondary sources for context, chronology, and interviews.
4. Blogs, forums, social media, and general reference pages only as leads unless independently confirmed.

## Process
1. Translate the topic into 5–12 answerable research questions.
2. Define time period, geography, terms, and what the documentary is not trying to prove.
3. Search for primary evidence and contrary evidence, not only sources that support a preferred angle.
4. Record source ID, title, author/institution, publication date, access date, canonical URL, source type, relevant page/section/quote paraphrase, supported claims, and limitations.
5. Split compound claims into atomic rows in `claim_register.csv`.
6. Build a chronological timeline and entity glossary.
7. Track unresolved questions and conflicting accounts explicitly.
8. Update the dossier if the topic is time-sensitive; never treat an old source as proof of a current state.

## Claim states
`verified`: directly supported by credible evidence; `supported-debated`: evidence exists but interpretation/conflict remains; `hypothesis`: plausible but not established; `unknown`: evidence is insufficient; `false-misleading`: contradicted or materially distorted.

## Research handoff
Provide a concise thesis-neutral brief, strongest evidence, counter-evidence, uncertainty notes, suggested visual evidence, and claim IDs suitable for script citation.
